A closer look at recent projects I have built. Each write-up walks through the problem, the solution, and the lessons learned along the way.

Campus Prediction Markets 2026 · Founder & Builder

John Street

Problem

WashU students heavily rely on platforms like Sidechat as their primary campus application. There was a missing opportunity to gamify the local campus discourse.

Solution

John Street serves as a replacement for Sidechat. Users can actively bet on campus events and engage with localized prediction markets.

Part 1

Understanding the problem

Students text about campus events constantly on existing platforms. I decided to introduce a prediction market directly into that social ecosystem.

Part 2

Coming up with ideas and solutions

I designed the app to function as the definitive campus hub. It combines standard social mechanics with interactive betting odds for university-specific occurrences.

Part 3

Deciding & Prototyping

The initial focus was entirely on getting the market mechanics correct. I built out the core trading engine and prepared it for a closed trial.

Part 4

Round of testing and improvements

We launched the Beta to 40 initial testers. The platform successfully facilitated over 100 trades during this testing phase.

Reflections

The biggest takeaway was the necessity of mobile optimization. Building for the web created friction, so the current active development is heavily optimized for a mobile experience.

AI UX 2026 · Developer

LLM Slack Interface

Problem

When people creating conversations ask follow-up questions or seek clarification on specific outputs, those interactions clutter the primary chat window. This makes it difficult for both the creator and the people reviewing the conversation to identify a clear final answer.

Solution

A Slack-style UI change solves this chat pollution. Highlighting text and clicking a button opens the follow-up in a right-hand sidebar thread instead of the main conversation.

Part 1

Understanding the problem

As a workaround for chat pollution, people often ask the model to summarize the entire conversation or migrate to a new chat window. The main conversation gets lost in the noise.

Part 2

Coming up with ideas and solutions

We accepted that follow-up questions are a permanent part of the workflow and gave them a dedicated space. This keeps the root conversation clean. All exploratory back-and-forth stays isolated and addressable.

Part 3

Deciding & Prototyping

We built context-aware thread routing to support the isolated chats. This passes the global session context alongside the highlighted text and the user's sidebar prompt to the backend.

Part 4

Round of testing and improvements

We added a reply count badge on the parent block in the main chat to display the number of messages inside its thread. Users can easily locate branching conversations.

Reflections

Success is defined by fewer context-reset sessions and faster review times during handoffs. Strong thread adoption is also a key indicator of a resolved user workflow.
